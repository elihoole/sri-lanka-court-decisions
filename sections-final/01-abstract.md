# Abstract 

**Key Insights:**

*The SriLankaCourtDecisions corpus on writs and fundamental rights judgements delivered 2012 to 2022, reveals that:*

1. *The Court of Appeal delivers nearly six times more judgments than the Supreme Court annually, yet, its jurisprudence remains systematically understudied;*  
2. *Numerically, judgements on writ petitions vastly outnumber judgements on fundamental rights petitions;*  
3. *Only 31 from the 1741 judgements on writ petitions from the Court of Appeal were appealed against to Supreme Court, suggesting that at a practical level the Court of Appeal functions effectively as the first and final arbiter of these petitions;*  
4. *The Supreme Court takes an average of 4.17 years to resolve Fundamental Rights and Court of Appeal resolves writs faster (mean 3.15 years);*  
5. *Land disputes and state employment matters are the dominant themes in appellate jurisprudence;*   
6. *The linguistic patterns and decision length  in these judgements confirm, empirically, doctrinal hierarchies. Judgements by the Supreme Court are lengthier and more complex in terms of linguistic patterns; and*  
7. *At a surface level, institutional stability and number of judgements reveal a relationship that requires further investigation within the Supreme Court as well as when comparing the Supreme Court with the Court of Appeal.* 

**Keywords:** empirical legal research; computational methods; Sri Lanka; Supreme Court; Court of Appeal; fundamental rights jurisdiction; writ jurisdiction; access to justice.
