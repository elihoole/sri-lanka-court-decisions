# Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the \texttt{SriLankaCourtDecisions} corpus – a text corpus containing 1278 Supreme Court decisions and 7314 Court of Appeal decisions from Sri Lanka. We also presented some descriptive statistics on the performance of the country’s apex courts, identified most salient words in the corpus, and modelled most common topics and their word distributions. As mentioned at the beginning, the primitive stage of digitalisation of Sri Lanka’s legal system necessarily entails that the work of this paper, although a significant contribution in the compilation and sharing of the decision text corpus and initial insights, is also preliminary in nature. The computational methods used even at this stage, however, have yielded findings demonstrative of the functioning of the superior courts of Sri Lanka – often exposing or confirming trends within the justice system that merit further investigation, and in some cases, urgent solutions. For instance, the low ratio of Supreme Court appeals to Court of Appeal writ decisions (31:1,741 during 2012-2022) confirms empirically that the Court of Appeal serves as the practical forum for writ remedies, a structural reality obscured by doctrinal scholarship’s focus on Supreme Court precedent. While the immediate next steps in relation to the corpus and computational analysis are clear and have been detailed above, they serve then not just to explore other features of the decision-making process in the Superior Courts, but also to further build on and explore some of the findings already highlighted in this paper.

The patterns identified in this study are preliminary but sufficient to warrant attention in both scholarly analysis and reform debates. The research directions outlined here are neither exhaustive nor speculative – they respond directly to gaps revealed by the empirical analysis of the \texttt{SriLankaCourtDecisions} corpus and address limitations in data, method, and scope that constrain present conclusions. Pursuing these directions would advance understanding of how appellate courts function, what disputes they resolve, and how judicial work relates to broader questions of access to justice, institutional design, and the rule of law.
